About

I'm drawn to problems where the data comes from the physical world: space, physics, geology, and anything else where the measurements are hard to get. I'm most interested in the edge of what we can currently measure and predict, and I try to be just as clear about where a model stops being trustworthy.

Before school I served in the U.S. Army as an airborne infantry mortarman with the 1-501st Parachute Infantry Regiment in Alaska. There, real-time data, precise calculation and clear communication under pressure had real consequences. I bring the same discipline to data work: check the inputs, know the error bars, and make the output something a person can act on.

Featured project

Three spacecraft, one solar wind

Real-time data pipeline · updates every 30 minutes

NOAA's new SOLAR-1 satellite went operational in June 2026, joining NASA's IMAP and ACE at the Sun–Earth L1 point. For the first time, three spacecraft stream live solar wind data side by side. This pipeline ingests all three, archives the history, and turns it into decisions:

  • Physics-derived features: ram pressure, solar wind–magnetosphere coupling, and the warning time before the wind reaches Earth
  • Anomaly detection (Isolation Forest) to flag shocks and solar storm arrivals
  • Cross-spacecraft comparison: calibration bias, correlation, and timing lag between instruments
  • Kafka streaming layer that raises alerts as each minute of data arrives
